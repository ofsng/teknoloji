# Advanced Materials for Soft Pneumatic Actuators: From Elastomers to Smart Multifunctional Composites

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Soft pneumatic actuators are key components of new soft robotic systems due to their compliant deformability, light weight and adaptable interactions. Their use is, however, limited by a constant antagonism between softness, force generation, dimensional stability, fatigue resistance, airtightness, manufacture and functional integration. In this critical review, advanced materials used for soft pneumatic actuators are discussed based on a material-processing-structure-performance paradigm. Conductive, self-healing, variable-stiffness, magnetoactive and fiber-reinforced, textile-based, architected, and silicone-based and polyurethane-based composites are compared to conventional silicone, polyurethane, and thermoplastic elastomers. Special emphasis is placed on the deformation, blocked force, pneumatic efficiency, hysteresis, leakage and failure control by matrix chemistry, reinforcement orientation, interfacial adhesion, filler dispersion, crosslinking and fabrication-induced defects. During the assessment, the authors also assess whether multifunctional additions provide a dependable level of sensing, repairing, reconfiguring or stiffness modulation with considerations of extensibility, density, durability, processability, and cost. The existing literature is then assessed for inconsistencies in material characterization, actuator geometry, pressure, fatigue protocols and performance normalization, which often make meaningful comparisons between studies difficult. Grippers and wearable and rehabilitation devices, biomedical systems, locomotion robots and haptic interfaces are considered based on the application-specific requirements. Lastly, the priorities for multiaxial characterization, interface-dominated design, standardized benchmarking, autonomous damage management, scalable multi-material manufacturing, and environmentally responsible material selection are proposed. The review also concludes that future progress will be based on integrated heterogeneous architectures that will enable a balance of mechanical output, compliance, reliability, intelligence, repairability and production scalability, rather than on further advances of increasingly soft elastomers.
