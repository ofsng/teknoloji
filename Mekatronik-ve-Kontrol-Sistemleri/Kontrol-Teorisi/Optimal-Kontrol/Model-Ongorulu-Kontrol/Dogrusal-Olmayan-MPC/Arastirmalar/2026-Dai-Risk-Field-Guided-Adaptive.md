# Risk Field-Guided Adaptive Nonlinear Model Predictive Control for ROV Path Tracking

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Near-hull inspection requires the tracking of a prescribed path while maintaining clearance from structural hazards. This numerical study combines geometry-based reference generation with nonlinear model predictive control. The controller adjusts the steering increment penalty according to position uncertainty and uses a Gaussian inequality to limit the summed hazard field at predicted positions. In the corrected five-path nominal tests, the original capture-gated controller and its matched-reference fixed-weight counterpart achieved mean original-path lateral RMSEs of 0.10625 and 0.10606m and mean minimum clearances of 0.39310 and 0.39060m, respectively. Scheduling reduced the noisy-segment steering rate RMS by 48.5% relative to the low-noise constant and reduced the low-noise curved-segment RMSE by 44.0% relative to the high-noise constant. The tracking error and fallback frequency increased relative to the low-noise constant, indicating a scenario-dependent trade-off. A global upper bound showed that geometric clearance alone kept the Gaussian field below its limit in the original separated layouts. In three overlapping layouts, both ablation arms satisfied the sampled clearance requirement, while adding the Gaussian inequality with a predictive margin of 0.008 reduced the peak sampled field intensity from 1.2837–1.3050 to 1.1451–1.1468. The findings apply to the tested numerical scenarios.
