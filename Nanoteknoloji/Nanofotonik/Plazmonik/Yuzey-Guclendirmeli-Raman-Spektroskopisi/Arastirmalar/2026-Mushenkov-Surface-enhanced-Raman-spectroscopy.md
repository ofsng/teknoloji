# Surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy on the membranes for antimicrobial resistance testing

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Introduction Antimicrobial resistance is a major global health threat, causing millions of deaths every year. Traditional antibiotic susceptibility tests rely on the detection of bacterial growth in the presence of antimicrobial agents. These tests take over 1-2 days to complete, so empirical therapy schemes often begin before the test results are available. Rapid tests for antimicrobial resistance are needed to optimize treatment for bacterial infections. A combination of the MTT test and Raman spectroscopy can provide a 1.5-h test for antimicrobial susceptibility, requiring 10 6 –10 8 CFU/mL of bacteria. Here the novel rapid antibiotic susceptibility test for trace amounts of bacteria is described. Methods Surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) has been suggested to improve the sensitivity of the test. SERS spectra of MTT-treated bacteria can be acquired using membranes coated with silver nanoparticles. These membranes simultaneously concentrate the sample on the surface and generate SERS spectra under 532-nm or 638-nm laser irradiation. Results Both the spectra of individual bacteria and the large-area measurements provided information for antimicrobial resistance testing. The bacterial titer can be decreased down to 500 CFU/mL in the test. The overall analysis time was 1.5 h. Discussion This rapid sensitive test highly promising for point-of-care applications avoiding durable bacterial culturing.
