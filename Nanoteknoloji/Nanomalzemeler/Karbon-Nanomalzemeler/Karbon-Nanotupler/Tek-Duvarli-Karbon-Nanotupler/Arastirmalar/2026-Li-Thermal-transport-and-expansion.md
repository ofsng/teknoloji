# Thermal transport and expansion behavior of vertically aligned single-walled carbon nanotube block reinforced copper matrix composites by finite element simulation

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> This study establishes quantitative relationships between interfacial condition, reinforcement orientation, and the directional thermophysical response for vertically aligned single-walled carbon nanotube (VASWCNT) block reinforced Cu matrix composites, providing a mechanistic basis for simultaneously enhancing heat dissipation and dimensional stability. An experimentally informed three-dimensional representative volume element finite element model was developed by incorporating the measured block geometry, volume fraction, orientation, and interfacial characteristics of both uncoated and Cu-coated composites. Cu-mediated interfacial coupling promoted more continuous heat and load transfer between the VASWCNT blocks and the Cu matrix, thereby enhancing thermal transport, improving deformation compatibility, and alleviating local stress and strain concentrations. Orientation analysis further demonstrated that the reinforcement orientation determines the directional utilization of the highly anisotropic thermal and thermomechanical properties of the VASWCNT blocks, thereby governing the effective thermal conductivity and thermal expansion of the composites. Among the evaluated models, the finite element predictions showed the closest agreement with the experimental measurements. These findings establish a quantitative basis for coordinated interface engineering and VASWCNT orientation control, providing a physics-based strategy for designing Cu matrix thermal management composites with simultaneously high thermal conductivity and low coefficient of thermal expansion.
