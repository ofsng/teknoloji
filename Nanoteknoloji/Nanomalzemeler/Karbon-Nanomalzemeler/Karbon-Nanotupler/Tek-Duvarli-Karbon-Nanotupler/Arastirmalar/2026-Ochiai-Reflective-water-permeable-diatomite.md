# Reflective, water-permeable diatomite masks enhancing output voltage of water-floating single-walled carbon nanotube thermoelectric generators

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Abstract Water-floating single-walled carbon nanotube (SWCNT) thermoelectric generators (TEGs) raise their own temperature gradient by evaporative cooling and therefore run without a heat source or a heat sink. Earlier work enlarged this small gradient by feeding more water to the cooling regions, where the water evaporates. Sunlight also warms the cooling regions, so reflecting it away opens a second route to a larger gradient. We compared four devices covering combinations of reflectivity and water permeability on the cooling regions: an unmasked reference, a white acrylic resin mask, a two-layer acrylic + diatomite mask, and a diatomite mask. Under artificial sunlight of 1000 W/m 2 , the diatomite-masked device gave an output voltage of 0.50 mV, against 0.25 mV for the unmasked device. Two thickness-matched device pairs locate the origin of this gain. Covering the bare film with a thin reflective mask raised the output voltage by 0.09 mV, net of the evaporation it costs. A water-permeable diatomite mask raised it by a further 0.16 mV over a sealed one. Junction thermocouples on replicate devices agree with the derived temperature differences. A cooling-side layer that reflects sunlight and conducts water thus offers a route to larger gradients in devices that raise their own.
