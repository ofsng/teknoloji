# Engineering and Probing Atomic Defects in Two-Dimensional Transition Metal Dichalcogenides

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Atomic defects in two-dimensional transition metal dichalcogenides (2D TMDs) endow the materials with unique physical and chemical properties, promoting their potential applications in optoelectronic devices, quantum information technology, and high-efficiency catalysis. However, precise control over defect formation and reliable atomic-scale characterization remain major challenges for translating defect functionalities into practical applications. In this review, we begin with a concise overview of typical spontaneous atomic defects in as-grown 2D TMDs. We then summarize engineering strategies for introducing artificial atomic defects, including vacuum annealing, chemical doping, particle beam irradiation, and atomic manipulation. Next, we review recent progress on the characterization of the atomic structure, electronic and magnetic properties of individual point defects, as well as defect–defect interactions in multi-defect systems, with particular emphasis on scanning probe microscopy techniques. Finally, we discuss current challenges and future opportunities in controllable defect synthesis, advanced characterization, and defect-enabled device applications in 2D TMD systems.
