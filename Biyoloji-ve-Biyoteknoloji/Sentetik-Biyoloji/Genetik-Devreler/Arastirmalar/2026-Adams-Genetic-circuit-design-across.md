# Genetic circuit design across species and contexts: progress and future directions

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Synthetic biology enables programmable control of microbial behavior. This has diverse applications across many sectors of the economy. For nearly a quarter century, synthetic biologists have reported ever more powerful and complex genetic systems in the model bacterium Escherichia coli. This has been driven by composable parts that perform as expected in well-defined laboratory conditions, which creates a positive feedback loop of increasing design complexity. While impressive, composability within one host does not by itself enable portability–predictably moving functional designs between host organisms and conditions. Yet, portability is essential for real-world applications. The lack of portability limits current practice - essentially circuits are re-engineered to suit each new host and context. However, a growing body of recent work has hinted that there may be generalizable rules for transferring parts and designs from E. coli to other bacteria. Thus, this review describes recent advances in the design and implementation of genetic circuits in diverse bacterial hosts and environmental conditions, highlighting key considerations for a new era of engineering portable genetic designs.
