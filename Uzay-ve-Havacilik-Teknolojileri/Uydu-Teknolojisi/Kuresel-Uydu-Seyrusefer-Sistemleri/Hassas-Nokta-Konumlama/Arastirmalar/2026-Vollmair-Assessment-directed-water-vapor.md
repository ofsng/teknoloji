# Assessment of directed water vapor radiometer-based tropospheric corrections in GNSS precise point positioning

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Abstract Deficiencies in modelling of the signal propagation delay induced by the troposphere are one of the primary error sources in high-precision GNSS applications. The wet part of the troposphere exhibits significant spatial and temporal variations and therefore cannot be adequately described by models. Conventional GNSS processing strategies, rely on the estimation of tropospheric parameters. These parameters are strongly correlated with the station height and receiver clock, which limits the achievable precision and temporal resolution of the parameters estimated. In this study alternative approaches which fully corrects the tropospheric delay without estimating tropospheric parameters are tested. For this purpose, Water-Vapor-Radiometer-based, raytracing based, and model-based tropospheric corrections are used within a Precise Point Positioning analysis. A classic Precise Point Positioning analysis with troposphere parameter estimation is used as a reference. Evaluation is based on the repeatability of station coordinates and comparison of receiver clock offsets. The results show that Water-Vapor-Radiometer sky-scan measurements capture significant asymmetric tropospheric effects that are not represented by classical models. Under rain-free conditions, the sky-scan approach achieves repeatability of the height component comparable to that of classical Precise Point Positioning solutions with troposphere parameter estimation. The results further indicate that rainfall currently represents the main limitation of Water-Vapor-Radiometer based corrections. Overall, the study demonstrates that directional tropospheric observations provide additional information beyond conventional zenith-delay parameterizations and may contribute to future measurement-based tropospheric correction approaches in high-precision geodetic applications.
