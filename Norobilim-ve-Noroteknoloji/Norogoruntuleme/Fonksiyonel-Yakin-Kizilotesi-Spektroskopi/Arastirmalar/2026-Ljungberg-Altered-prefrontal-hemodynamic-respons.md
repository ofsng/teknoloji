# Altered prefrontal hemodynamic response during a working memory task in individuals with psychotic spectrum disorders and a history of aggression measured by functional near-infrared spectroscopy

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Introduction Aggressive acts committed by individuals with psychotic spectrum disorders (PSD) account for a small proportion of societal violence but may contribute to stigma. We examined prefrontal hemodynamic responses during visuospatial working memory (WM) in individuals with PSD and a history of aggression (PSD+AGG; n = 76), individuals with PSD without such a history (PSD−AGG; n = 66), and healthy controls (HC; n = 85). Methods Oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) responses were measured using 16-channel functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) during a Reverse Corsi Block-Tapping Test. Hemodynamic responses were analyzed using performance-modulated and non-amplitude-modulated (NA) generalized linear models. Performance-modulated models incorporated block-level WM performance into task regressors, whereas NA models estimated task-related responses without behavioral amplitude modulation. Multiple comparisons were controlled using cluster-based permutation family-wise error rate (FWER) correction. Results In the performance-modulated Corsi contrast (V.1), PSD+AGG showed a lower HbO response than PSD−AGG in right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) channels 15–16 (cluster p = 0.049), with no significant clusters relative to HC. No significant clusters were observed in the performance-modulated Hard-versus-Easy or NA Corsi contrasts. In the NA Hard-versus-Easy contrast (V.4), PSD+AGG showed lower right medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) responses than PSD−AGG (channels 9–12, cluster p = 0.0002). Relative to HC, PSD+AGG showed higher responses in left dlPFC channel 3 and left mPFC channels 6–7, and a mixed-direction right prefrontal cluster: lower responses in channels 9–12 and higher responses in channels 13–16. PSD−AGG did not differ significantly from HC. Post-hoc group × WAIS-IV Matrix Reasoning interactions, significant after false discovery rate correction, challenged the assumption of homogeneous covariate slopes in both V.4 comparisons. Discussion V.1 provides the principal finding of altered performance-related right dlPFC recruitment in PSD+AGG. Given these interactions and the potential overlap between WM and reasoning processes, the V.4 findings should be considered exploratory and may reflect illness-related neurocognitive differences rather than differences specifically attributable to aggression history. Specificity to aggression history and a possible contribution of illness severity remain uncertain.
