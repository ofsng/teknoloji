# The influence of the 1/f component in non-invasive transcranial electrical stimulation waveforms on neural oscillations

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Background Transcranial alternating current stimulation applies a sinusoidal waveform at a constant frequency, which can modulate brain function by entraining neural oscillations. Recently, transcranial electrical stimulation (tES) using complex, personalized stimulus waveforms derived from individual neural dynamics has been shown to increase motor cortex excitability and improve visuospatial attention. However, the underlying mechanisms of this novel tES remain unclear. Electroencephalography (EEG) signals contain periodic components corresponding to neural oscillations and aperiodic components represented by the 1/f component. This study investigated the role of the 1/f component in tES based on neural dynamics. We hypothesized that tES reproducing an individual’s resting-state EEG spectrum, including the 1/f component, would modulate periodic and aperiodic components more effectively than tES without the 1/f component. Methods In this randomized, sham-controlled, crossover study, 21 healthy young adults (20 included in the analysis) received three conditions: individual spectrum-characteristic stimulation (ISS), aperiodic component-removed stimulation (ARS), and sham stimulation. ISS reproduced an individual’s EEG characteristics during the eyes-closed resting state, whereas ARS used a waveform with the 1/f component removed. The prominent periodic spectral components for ARS and ISS were in the alpha range. Each stimulation was applied for 20 min. We recorded resting-state EEG before and after the intervention, and then evaluated alpha power, the aperiodic exponent and offset. Intervention effects were analyzed using generalized linear mixed models with Holm–Bonferroni-corrected post hoc contrasts. Results ARS significantly increased alpha power compared with sham, whereas ISS did not differ significantly from sham. The direct comparison between ARS and ISS was also not significant. Neither ARS nor ISS significantly altered the aperiodic exponent or offset compared with sham. Conclusions Contrary to our expectations, ARS enhanced alpha oscillations relative to sham, whereas ISS had no significant effect. Although the two personalized protocols did not differ significantly from each other, these results suggest that the spectral composition of personalized tES waveforms, including the presence of the 1/f component, may be a relevant parameter warranting further investigation. These findings provide a basis for optimizing personalized tES waveforms. This personalized tES approach may contribute to the future development of neuromodulation-based rehabilitation strategies.
