# Seasonal Evaluation of Nutrient Removal from Simulated Wastewater Using Aquatic Plants in South Texas

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Floating treatment wetlands (FTWs) are an effective nature-based solution for mitigating excess nutrient accumulation in the aquatic system. However, nutrient removal efficiency can vary significantly among plant species and seasonal conditions due to differences in plant growth, metabolism, and environmental stress response. In this study, we evaluated the nutrient removal potential of four floating plant species, including common rush (CR, Juncus effusus), three-square bulrush (TSB, Schoenoplectus pungens), tiger grass (TG, Centella asiatica), and switchgrass (SG, Panicum virgatum), during winter and spring conditions in South Texas using controlled and replicated bucket-scale experiments. Temporal reductions in total nitrogen (TN) and total phosphorus (TP) were monitored under standardized nutrient-loading conditions, and nutrient removal performance was quantified based on both concentration reduction and biomass-normalized removal. Nutrient-removal patterns differed between the winter and spring runs, and the late winter concentration increases may reflect nutrient release associated with plant stress. Despite these seasonal limitations, FTWs maintained measurable nutrient-removal capacity during the analyzed winter periods. Among the tested species, CR exhibited the highest biomass-normalized TN removal in both seasons and the highest biomass-normalized TP removal in winter; SG had the highest biomass-normalized TP removal in spring. These findings highlight the potential of FTWs for year-round nutrient management in southern US climates and identify CR as a resilient and effective species for nutrient remediation applications under variable seasonal conditions.
