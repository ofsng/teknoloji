# Chlorination-driven enrichment of Firmicutes in a full-scale drinking water treatment system revealed by 16 S rRNA gene sequencing

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Drinking water treatment systems (DWTS) strongly shape microbial communities by imposing physicochemical and disinfectant-driven selective pressures. However, the ecological mechanisms underlying microbial persistence and enrichment during drinking water treatment remain poorly understood, particularly in tropical regions. In this study, we investigated bacterial community dynamics across a full-scale drinking water treatment system (DWTS) in southeastern Brazil, evaluating raw water (RW), post-treatment water (PTW), and tap water (TW) during rainy and dry seasons using 16 S rRNA gene sequencing and quantitative PCR. Marked differences in bacterial community composition were observed between raw and treated water samples. RW was dominated by Proteobacteria, particularly Betaproteobacteria, whereas Firmicutes, mainly members of the class Bacilli, became dominant in PTW and TW, reaching relative abundances above 90% in the dry season. Genera affiliated with Geobacillus, Bacillus, Paenibacillus, and Enterococcus persisted throughout the system and increased proportionally after chlorination. Alpha diversity and total bacterial 16 S rRNA gene copy numbers decreased markedly following treatment, confirming effective biomass reduction but indicating strong ecological selection. Several genera that include opportunistic pathogens (e.g., Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas, Mycobacterium, Enterococcus) were detected at low relative abundance in treated water; however, species-level identification and viability cannot be inferred from amplicon sequencing alone. Our findings suggest that drinking water treatment exerts selective pressure favoring resistant Gram-positive taxa, particularly spore-forming Firmicutes. These results contribute to understanding microbial ecology in chlorinated drinking water systems in tropical regions.
