# From waste management to carbon removal: Assessing municipal solid waste-based BECCS options in the UAE

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Municipal solid waste (MSW) treatment systems are increasingly deployed to address waste management challenges, yet their potential contribution to engineered carbon dioxide removal remains underexplored. This study evaluates the techno-economic performance of two MSW-based pathways capable of delivering negative emissions: thermal incineration with post-combustion carbon capture and storage (INC-CCS) and landfill gas recovery with biomethane upgrading and CO 2 storage (BMU-CCS). Four configurations (INC, INC-CCS, BMU, BMU-CCS) are compared under consistent assumptions for waste throughput, plant performance, and financial parameters calibrated to UAE conditions. Results show that both CCS-enabled pathways achieve net negative emissions, but with markedly different characteristics. INC-CCS provides the largest removal potential (−156 ktCO 2 /y) but at a higher cost (131 US$ 2023 /tCO 2 removed) due to capital requirements and efficiency penalties, increasing electricity costs from 70 to 194 US$ 2023 /MWh. In contrast, BMU-CCS delivers smaller removal volumes (−14.1 ktCO 2 /y) but at a lower cost (51.3 US$ 2023 /tCO 2 removed), reflecting the high purity of the CO 2 stream generated during upgrading and the limited additional infrastructure required. These cost values represent plant-gate capture or separation and CO 2 compression costs and exclude downstream CO 2 transport and geological storage; they should therefore not be interpreted as full-chain CCS or permanent carbon-removal costs. Nevertheless, the findings highlight a fundamental trade-off between the scale of removal and its cost in waste-based bioenergy with carbon capture and storage (BECCS) systems. The results suggest that MSW treatment infrastructure can contribute meaningfully to net-zero strategies, particularly in regions with limited biomass resources but growing waste streams. While calibrated to the UAE, the framework and insights are applicable to rapidly urbanizing regions worldwide.
