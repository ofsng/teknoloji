# Advances in Nanomaterials and Green Chemistry for Sustainable Chemical Engineering: Applications in Environmental Remediation and Energy Storage

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Chemical engineering faces a growing need to balance the efficiency of the chemical industry with the need to reduce environmental pollution and resource depletion and to move toward low-carbon energy systems. Conventional treatment and energy-storage technologies are typically based on energy-intensive processes, limited raw materials, or toxic agents and products whose end-of-life effects are not well understood. The study critically explores the role of innovations in nanomaterials and green chemistry in more sustainable chemical engineering, focusing on environmental remediation and energy storage. The research design is qualitative, interpretivist, exploratory and documentary, and it examines peer-reviewed journal articles, review papers, scholarly books, institutional publications and policy literature that have been published primarily between 2020 and 2026, together with earlier seminal works. Secondary evidence was synthesized using qualitative thematic analysis, which allowed the identification of recurring scientific claims, technological pathways, limitations and research priorities. The results show that engineered nanomaterials such as metal and metal-oxide nanoparticles, carbon-based materials, nanocomposites, and biomass-derived composites are potential nanotechnological tools for adsorption, photocatalytic degradation, membrane separation, contaminant detection, and water, wastewater, air and soil remediation. Nanostructured electrodes, electrolytes, separators and interfacial materials are useful in energy storage to enhance the performance of batteries, supercapacitors and emerging energy storage devices. The review reveals that these advantages depend on green synthesis methods, responsible material design, scalable production, recyclability and thorough evaluation of nanomaterial toxicity and life cycle effects. The study makes the case for incorporating principles of sustainable innovation in research, development and deployment, including the integration of green chemistry principles, safe-and-sustainable-by-design approaches, resource efficiency and lifecycle thinking. The implications include the need for a more precautionary and systems-based approach to the development of environmental and energy technologies enabled by nanomaterials.
