# Geotechnical assessment of the effects of blast-induced ground vibrations on structural integrity and open-pit slope stability

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Blast-induced ground vibrations in surface mining threaten nearby structures and open-pit slope stability. At the Koidu Mine in Sierra Leone, evidence-based mitigation is needed to protect community infrastructure while preserving slope integrity. This study evaluates the effectiveness of trenching in reducing vibration transmission using numerical simulations in ANSYS LS-DYNA. Four trench depths (0, 10, 16, and 22 m) are assessed against a fixed blast depth of 15 m. The baseline no-trench scenario yielded a peak particle velocity (PPV) of 7.94 mm/s at the nearest surface structure (50 m from the blasthole), below the USBM RI 8507 limit of 12.7 mm/s but exceeding the stricter DIN 4150-3 guideline of 5 mm/s for sensitive structures. Trenching reduced PPV by 46%, 62%, and 66% at depths of 10, 16, and 22 m, respectively, with 16 m identified as the effective optimum due to diminishing returns. These reductions improve foundation-level vibration safety for surface buildings. In contrast, slope stability analyses performed under undrained conditions with the blast vibration applied as a dynamic load showed that the Factor of Safety (FoS) remained below 1.0 in all trench configurations (0.101-0.105). Trench depth therefore had no practical influence on slope stability: the slope failed under blast loading at every depth tested, and the critical PPV threshold lies below the lowest value examined (2.68 mm/s). Consequently, while trenching remains effective for mitigating structural vibration, it is insufficient to guarantee slope stability. An integrated approach combining trenching with drainage systems, ground improvement techniques, or adjusted blast charge designs is therefore required for effective risk management at the Koidu Mine.
