# Modern Breeding in Mungbean (Vigna radiata)- The Role of Marker-assisted Selection and Genomic Tools: A Review

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Pulse crops such as chickpea, pigeonpea, lentil, mungbean and urdbean serve as a vital source of dietary protein, micronutrients and agricultural sustainability. Mungbean (Vigna radiata L. wilczek) is a short-season, protein-rich legume critical for food security and soil fertility. However, mungbean suffers from stagnant yields due to biotic and abiotic stresses, limited genetic improvement and slow breeding cycles. Its yields are low ranging from 0.5-1.5/ t/ha due to biotic stresses caused by mungbean yellow mosaic virus disease, bruchids and abiotic stresses including drought, heat, salinity etc. Marker-assisted selection (MAS) has revolutionized pulse breeding by enabling precise, efficient and accelerated development of new cultivars with tolerance to stresses and improved agronomic traits. The review covers the traditional QTL mapping and marker-assisted selection (MAS) for key traits, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and genotyping-by-sequencing (GBS) for high-resolution trait dissection and genomic selection. The recent genome sequencing and large germplasm collections enables the identification of trait-linked SNPs. The review also discusses cutting-edge genome editing specifically CRISPR/Cas9, used to knock out viral genes including AV1, AC1 conferring resistance to yellow mosaic virus. This review highlights the future directions on integrated role of GWAS-CRISPR, epigenetics, High throughput screening that can dramatically shorten breeding cycles and helpful to produce superior mungbean cultivars. This integrative review aims to guide plant breeders and geneticists in applying genomic and editing technologies to sustainably improve mungbean and related legumes.
