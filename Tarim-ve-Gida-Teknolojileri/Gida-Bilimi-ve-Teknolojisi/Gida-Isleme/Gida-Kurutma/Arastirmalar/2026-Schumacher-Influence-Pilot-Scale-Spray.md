# Influence of Pilot-Scale Spray- and Freeze-Drying on the Compositional, Structural, and Functional Properties of Rapeseed Protein Concentrate

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Rapeseed (Brassica napus L.) press cake, a side stream of oil production, has gained interest as a source of functional protein. While its processing has mainly been studied at laboratory scale, a systematic comparison of pilot-scale spray- and freeze-drying of rapeseed protein concentrate (RPC) is lacking. This study therefore compared RPC (~53% protein) from one pilot-scale extraction batch, dried by pilot-scale spray-drying (SD) or freeze-drying (FD). No differences were detected in the SDS-PAGE pattern, secondary structure (FTIR, α-helix:β-sheet ratio 0.49 vs. 0.52), or apparent isoelectric point (pH ~3.5). Physical properties, in contrast, differed: FD had lower residual moisture (2.7 vs. 7.1%) and coarser, bimodally distributed particles (D3,2 61.5 vs. 22.6 µm), whereas SD was lighter (L* 69.8 vs. 62.8). FD showed higher water- and oil-holding capacities (+13% and +137%), whereas SD was more soluble at pH 3-4. Emulsifying and foaming properties and the least gelation concentration (17%) did not differ significantly. Under the studied conditions, the drying method mainly affected physical powder properties, making pilot-scale spray-drying an alternative to freeze-drying when a lighter, finer powder is targeted.
