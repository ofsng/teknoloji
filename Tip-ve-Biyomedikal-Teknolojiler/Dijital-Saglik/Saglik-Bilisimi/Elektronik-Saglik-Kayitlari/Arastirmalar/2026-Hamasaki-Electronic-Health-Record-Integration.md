# Electronic Health Record Integration and Usability of External Clinical Information Among US Office-Based Physicians: A Cross-Sectional Study

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Background/Objectives: Digital health is expected to improve care; however, its value depends on integrating information into clinical practice. Physicians increasingly receive information from outside their organisations, yet whether the way it arrives affects their ability to use it remains unclear. Methods: This cross-sectional study used the 2024 National Electronic Health Records Survey, a nationally representative survey of office-based physicians in the United States. The outcome was reporting external clinical information as “Not at all” easy to use. The exposure was its frequency of integration into the physician’s electronic health record. Survey-weighted logistic regression models were adjusted for physician-, practice- and insurance-related characteristics, and adjusted predicted probabilities were estimated. Results: Of 1725 respondents, 1526 were analysed, representing 366,840 physicians. External information often or sometimes arrived as a scanned document for 86.9% of physicians and was often or sometimes integrated within the electronic health record for 47.5%. Although 84.1% agreed that electronic exchange improves quality of care, 89.2% reported difficulty finding important information, and 12.3% of those who answered the usability item could not use external information at all. Compared with physicians for whom information was often integrated, the adjusted odds of being unable to use it were higher when integration occurred sometimes (odds ratio [OR] = 5.37; 95% CI, 2.28–12.67), rarely (OR = 10.27; 95% CI, 4.23–24.91) and never (OR = 19.12; 95% CI, 8.01–45.64). The adjusted probability of being unable to use external information was 1.9% when integration occurred often and 24.7% when it never occurred. Conclusions: Physicians value electronic information exchange, but received information is often difficult to use. As reported by the same physicians, integration into electronic health records, as opposed to transmission alone, was associated with usability. Digital health may require greater attention to how information reaches clinicians.
