# Uncovering the erratic behaviour of heat and reaction fronts in thermochemical energy storage with non-stoichiometric oxides

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Gas-solid redox reactions of metal oxides and metals offer an efficient but understudied avenue for storing energy. Using reduction as an energy sink, then re-oxidation for energy release, charging and discharging steps arrive, building a cyclic, thermochemical energy storage (TCES) system. Perovskite metal oxides, like SrFeO 3–δ (SFO), withstand the required redox reactions whilst maintaining their perovskite structure, thus offering unmatched mechanical stability for TCES. Yet, a key technical challenge remains in that the reaction extent in perovskite oxides corresponds to continuous changes in oxygen non-stoichiometry (3-δ), adding a new degree of freedom in the thermodynamic description of reactions with perovskites, compounded with the partial pressure of oxygen ( p O 2 ) and process temperature ( T ). Here, we propose how to track the temperature and oxygen non-stoichiometry of SFO upon pressure- or temperature-induced redox reactions, treating SFO as a solid mixture represented by a single phase with variation in (3-δ), and by making analogies with adsorption–desorption columns. We depict how energy and oxygen traverse through the reacting SFO bed, highlighting smeared reduction front under p O 2 -induced charging and the creation of single or double reaction fronts in discharging. We then propose an approach in using non-stoichiometric oxides for remarkably efficient energy storage cycles, operating under a T -swing configuration. Applying SFO then leads to complete reversibility of the charge–discharge cycle and more than 20 % increase in capacity relative to inert solids. Understanding such improvement is made possible by embracing the redox flexibility and perovskites’ non-stoichiometry, features that hindered earlier attempts in using perovskite oxides for TCES.
