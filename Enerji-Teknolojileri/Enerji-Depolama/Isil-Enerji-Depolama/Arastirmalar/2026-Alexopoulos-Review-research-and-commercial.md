# Review of research and commercial solar concentrated systems for process heat with thermal energy storage in Greece and Cyprus

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Solar concentrating heating systems with thermal energy storage for supplying thermal power to industrial consumers are becoming increasingly important. Worldwide, dozens of such plants, predominantly parabolic trough systems, are already installed. In Greece and the Republic of Cyprus, two countries with high solar resources, such systems are also installed and operated. This paper presents a review with an analytical description of existing concentrated solar thermal (CST) systems for process heat applications operating at temperatures above 150 °C, with a focus on installations in Greece and Cyprus. For this purpose, a wide range of Internet databases, other sources, and scientific papers were reviewed. Overall, two commercial CST installations and four R&D or demonstration CST projects were identified in Greece and Cyprus. The two operational commercial plants represent a combined installed thermal capacity of 645 kWth. Both commercial installations are based on parabolic trough collector technology and operate at a maximum of 160 °C. Thermal energy storage is integrated only in research and demonstration projects, one of which currently includes large-scale storage. In the research and demonstration sector, CST systems are also being investigated for combined heat, cooling, and electricity generation at operating temperatures up to 400 °C. A comparison of the reviewed CST systems in Greece and Cyprus demonstrates several common trends. Commercial installations to date are generally small- to medium-scale systems focused on industrial heat supply at temperatures below 160 °C. In contrast, research and demonstration projects increasingly target higher operating temperatures of up to 400 °C and integrate advanced concepts such as thermal energy storage and combined cooling, heating, and power generation. The largest CST plant in Greece, which uses PTC technology, produces 5.75 MWth and is installed at the Athenian Brewery in Patras. Parabolic trough collectors dominate most identified installations, while Fresnel collector technology appears mainly in experimental applications. Furthermore, as thermal energy storage is deployed only in research and demonstration-oriented projects, this indicates that storage integration is still at an early stage of commercial deployment in the region. Compared with the Heineken brewery in Seville, Spain, which has a PTC field with a nominal output of 30 MWth, and pressurised-water tank storage with a capacity of 69 MWhth, the installed systems in Greece and Cyprus are small, with the exception of the CST plant installed at the Athenian Brewery. The R&D and demonstration plants cover up to 25% of the thermal energy demand. Only one of the two commercial plants covers 70% of the process thermal demand. Challenges and barriers were identified, and recommendations for overcoming these are given. Trends were identified based on the available data. Because reliable data on the economic performance of the examined projects are lacking, this aspect is not a focus of the paper.
