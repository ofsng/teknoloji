# LQR Robustness to Kite-Boundary and Fractional-Order Uncertainty in Linearized Airborne Wind Energy Tether Models

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Linear models of lateral tether vibration in airborne wind energy (AWE) systems usually treat the kite as a fixed boundary. This paper develops a multi-segment lateral tether model with geometric stiffness, coherent turbulence, and a ground-station LQR acting through a displacement-controlled fairlead carriage, and it relaxes the fixed boundary in two ways: the kite becomes a free mass-spring-damper node with swept stiffness, and its damping becomes a fractional-order (Caputo) operator. Although compliance lowers the fundamental frequency by up to 70%, a high-authority fixed-boundary gain re-used unmodified matches a boundary-aware redesign to within 1% at every node, and an integer-order gain matches a fractional-model-aware design at every damping order. Passivity explains this. The closed-loop tether is positive real at the boundary port, so stability holds for any passive kite boundary and any damping order 0<α≤1, while feedback reduces mid-span sensitivity to boundary forces about 400-fold. Three limits are found. With a realistic carriage servo, boundary-aware redesign lowers mid-span RMS by up to 22% for a highly compliant kite; distributed aerodynamic damping in crosswind flight is not absorbed; and absolute reductions depend on the discretization. The findings are robustness properties of the linear lateral model, not guarantees for a flying system.
