# Static-Torque Enhancement of Darrieus Vertical-Axis Wind Turbines with Dual-Airfoil Blades

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> The limited pre-starting torque of Darrieus-type vertical-axis wind turbines remains a major restriction for low-speed wind energy applications. This study evaluates, under fixed-rotor static conditions, two integrated dual-airfoil blade concepts derived from a baseline flow-concentrator geometry: the Static-Torque Booster Blade (STBB) and the Converging-Flow Blade (CFB). Both designs preserve the rotor envelope, blade height, radius, swept area, and NACA 6412 airfoil family, so that the effect of the relative arrangement between the primary torque-generating airfoil and the auxiliary flow-guiding airfoil can be isolated. Response surface methodology coupled with two-dimensional CFD was used only as a design-screening stage at U∞=7.0m/s to identify influential geometric parameters and reduce the design space; it is not interpreted as a quantitative predictor for the final comparison at U∞=2.8m/s. The selected configurations were then assessed through three-dimensional static CFD and wind tunnel static-torque measurements with the rotor fixed at prescribed azimuth positions under the same nominal confined low-speed condition. The two-dimensional screening showed that the auxiliary airfoil setting angle, azimuth position, and radial location strongly influence the static-torque response. In three dimensions, the STBB produced the highest static-torque peak (2.4×10−3Nm), increased the positive-torque fraction to f+=0.92, and yielded the lowest adverse-torque contribution. The CFB produced the highest mean positive torque contribution (1.174×10−3Nm), but also stronger adverse-torque sectors and no increase in f+ relative to the conventional rotor. Experimentally, the CFB reached approximately 0.0019–0.0020Nm near 0° and 180°, whereas the STBB reached approximately 0.0023Nm near 15–30°. The numerical–experimental comparison is therefore interpreted as trend-level static-torque agreement under confined wind tunnel conditions, not as a free-field performance prediction, a calibrated torque model, or a complete dynamic self-starting validation. Further free-rotation tests, mechanical loss characterization, and blockage correction studies are required before field-scale extrapolation.
