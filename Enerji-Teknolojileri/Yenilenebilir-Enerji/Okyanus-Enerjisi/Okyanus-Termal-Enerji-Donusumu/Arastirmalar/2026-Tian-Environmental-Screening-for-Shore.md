# Environmental Screening for Shore-Based Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion Site Selection: FVCOM Modeling of Copper Dispersion in the Xisha Sea

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Ocean thermal energy conversion (OTEC) offers a renewable energy option for remote islands. However, copper released from antifouling coatings on seawater pipelines may affect nearby marine ecosystems, while its potential environmental implications remain poorly understood. The main goal of this study is to examine copper dispersion from antifouling coatings on shore-based OTEC seawater pipelines and assess its implications for the selection of candidate sites in the Xisha Sea. A three-dimensional Finite-Volume Community Ocean Model (FVCOM) coupled with a tracer transport module was used to simulate incremental dissolved copper concentrations at three candidate sites during four representative months. The potential environmental implications were preliminarily evaluated by combining the simulated increments with literature-derived background dissolved copper concentrations and comparing the resulting estimates with DOC-adjusted ecological protection reference values. Results suggest that copper concentration increments are generally higher near the seabed and decrease with distance from the release sources, although signals can extend into shallower waters under certain conditions. The spatial extent of moderate concentration increments was generally smaller near Jinqing Island than near the other two sites, whereas broader areas of elevated increments were found near Zhaoshu Island under several reference scenarios. The assessment also indicates that background copper concentrations and DOC levels are important considerations when evaluating the potential environmental implications of copper release. The main conclusion is that pollutant dispersion modeling can provide supplementary environmental information for OTEC site selection. Although the site near Jinqing Island appears to be a potentially favorable candidate for further investigation, the results do not establish a definitive environmental ranking of the three sites. Site-specific measurements of dissolved copper and DOC are needed to better assess their environmental suitability.
