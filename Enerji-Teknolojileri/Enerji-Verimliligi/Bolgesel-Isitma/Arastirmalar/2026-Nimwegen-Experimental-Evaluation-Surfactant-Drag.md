# Experimental Evaluation of Surfactant Drag Reducing Agents for Use in District Heating and Cooling Networks

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Drag reducing agents (DRAs) can reduce the pressure losses in turbulent pipeflow in district heating and cooling networks (DHCNs), which can reduce the operational costs in current networks and allow for reduced investment, installation, and construction costs for future DHCNs. However, their hydraulic effectiveness, performance and stability at DHCN operating conditions remain insufficiently understood. In this work, measurements are performed at relevant conditions for DHCNs in a dedicated flow loop for different DRAs at temperatures of 20–115 °C. The results demonstrate that the DRAs are able to reduce the pressure drop under representative DHCNs operating temperatures by 50–80%. Furthermore, the tested DRAs exhibit stable drag reduction performance during week-long experiments and maintained chemical stability over month-long testing periods. However, the governing mechanisms for drag reduction also result in decreased heat transfer performance in heat exchanger experiments, highlighting a trade-off between hydraulic efficiency and thermal performance. These findings provide experimental evidence supporting the feasibility of DRAs for DHCN applications and form an important step towards future field trials and demonstrations.
