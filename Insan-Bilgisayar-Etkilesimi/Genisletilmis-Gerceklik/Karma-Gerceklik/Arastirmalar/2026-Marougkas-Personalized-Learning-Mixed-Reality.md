# Personalized Learning in Mixed Reality: A Systematic Review of Adaptive Feedback, Coaching, and AI in Higher and Professional Education

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Mixed reality (MR) presents potential to provide personalized learning through alignment of users’ activity with instructional assistance. Still, the nature of such adaptations and the educational value of personalization in this context have not been fully explored yet. This systematic review synthesizes 40 publication reports retrieved from Scopus (2016–2026) on personalized mixed reality in higher and professional education focusing on the aspects of educational context, technology configuration, means of adaptation, and learning outcomes. This review explores how individual performance, movements, gaze and dialogue affect the adjustment of feedback, coaching and practice. Five categories of personalized MR were revealed: coaching and rehearsing based on performance, feedback driven by sensors, eye-tracking-based attention management, guidance by an expert remotely connected through mixed reality and conversational or embodied artificial intelligence. Human coaching is prevalent among the evidence base, especially in teacher education, whereas recent research applies increasingly complex technology-assisted interventions in medicine, technology and other branches of higher education. The clearest reported benefits concern proximal teaching behaviors and procedural performance. Evidence for sustained unaided learning and authentic transfer remains limited. Bundled interventions require the careful attribution of outcomes to their individual components. The process of personalization was done mostly while performing current tasks or during sessions. There is no evidence available on persistent computational learner models across multiple sessions. This review summarizes current practices of personalized support in mixed reality and identifies a further research agenda of adaptive-learning-pathway design and evaluation.
