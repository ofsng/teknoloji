# Data-driven knowledge transfer and optimization for sustainable wire electrical discharge machining considering workpiece thickness effects and data scarcity

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Data scarcity and domain variability represent fundamental barriers to intelligent optimization in advanced manufacturing, where experimental data collection is costly and operating conditions frequently shift. This study proposes an application-integrated data-driven framework, combining surrogate augmentation, transfer learning, and multi-objective optimization, as the artificial intelligence (AI) contribution, validated on a data-scarce Wire Electrical Discharge Machining (WEDM) thickness-change scenario as the engineering application. Workpiece thickness variation is formulated as a domain shift problem, where knowledge learned from a well-characterized source domain (8 mm) is transferred to a data-scarce target domain (18 mm) with minimal experimentation. Experiments were conducted on tool steel workpieces to track five responses: surface roughness, material removal rate, kerf accuracy, tool wear rate, and carbon dioxide (CO 2 ) emissions. Parametric analysis revealed domain-specific behavioral shifts, where thicker workpieces yielded superior surface quality but higher energy consumption. Response Surface Methodology-based surrogate models were used to augment sparse experimental datasets with synthetically generated data points, enabling Artificial Neural Network pretraining. Transfer learning was utilized to fine-tune the pretrained networks to the target domain, showing favorable predictive performance for several responses under the smallest tested target-domain data budgets, but also not consistently outperforming benchmark models across all data regimes. The best-performing surrogate for each response was fed into a multi-objective optimizer, producing simulation-based Pareto-optimal solutions under varying productivity constraints. A surrogate-predicted Pareto-optimal solution improved on a representative experimental baseline, showing a potential of 7% reduction in surface roughness and a 32% reduction in CO 2 emissions, while maintaining comparable kerf accuracy.
