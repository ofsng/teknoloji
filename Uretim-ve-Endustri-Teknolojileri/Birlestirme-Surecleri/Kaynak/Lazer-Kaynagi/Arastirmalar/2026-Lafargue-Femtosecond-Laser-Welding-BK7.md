# Femtosecond Laser Welding of N-BK7 Glass: From In-Volume Modification to Large-Scale, High-Strength Joining

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Femtosecond laser welding enables the direct joining of transparent materials through localized energy deposition and melting at the interface. However, achieving high joint strength and welding over large areas remain challenging due to high thermal gradients generated during processing, which can lead to crack appearance. In this study, N-BK7 glass was irradiated using a femtosecond laser source emitting 500 fs pulses at 1030 nm to investigate the influence of energy deposition and temporal pulse distribution on laser-induced modifications. Repetitive single-pulse irradiation was compared to burst modes operating at 40 MHz and 1.28 GHz. In-volume modifications were characterized as a function of pulse or burst energy, scanning velocity, and the number of pulses per burst. The resulting modification morphologies were classified to establish process maps and identify parameter ranges that minimize defect formation and are suitable for welding. Based on these findings, the repetitive single-pulse mode was selected to weld N-BK7 glass assemblies over a diameter of 20 mm, including configurations with peripheral welds. Shear tests performed on smaller welded areas yielded a maximum shear strength of 640 MPa. These results demonstrate the potential of femtosecond laser welding for the assembly of large-scale optical components.
