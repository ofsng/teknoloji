# Prediction of 1-year corneal transplant failure using automated machine learning: a retrospective study using the NHS blood and transplant ocular tissue registry (OTAG Study 39)

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Identifying factors associated with successful corneal transplantation may help inform strategies for improving graft survival and visual outcomes. The failure rate in corneal transplantation is 5–10% at 1 year and 20–30% by year 3, with risk increasing over time. Previous large registry studies have identified risk factors for corneal graft failure using conventional statistical approaches, but the application of machine learning to large-scale transplant registry data remains relatively underexplored. Our study aimed to identify preoperative predictors of 1-year corneal graft failure using a large national registry and to evaluate the potential utility of machine learning for preoperative risk stratification. Using the NHS Blood and Transplant (NHSBT) Ocular Tissue Registry, comprising 43,008 first-eye, first-transplant procedures performed between 2000 and 2021, this study applied machine learning to identify preoperative predictors of 1-year transplant failure. Analyses were performed using complete case datasets and stratified by procedure type, indication for surgery, and feature importance. AutoGluon, an automated machine-learning framework, was used to develop predictive models, with model discrimination evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC). Complete case analysis included 15,199 first-eye, first-transplant procedures with 52 preoperative and operative variables (features). Transplant failure at 1 year occurred in 6.7% of cases, reflecting the expected class imbalance. The best-performing model achieved an AUC of 0.76. Surgery type was the strongest predictor of graft failure, followed by recipient age, surgeon case volume over the study period, transplant centre characteristics, preoperative visual acuity, and donor-recipient age difference. Models restricted to the ten most influential features demonstrated comparable performance (AUC = 0.73). The predictive models demonstrated moderate discriminatory performance, highlighting both the potential and limitations of automated machine learning for preoperative risk estimation. Surgery type, age-related factors, and surgeon- and centre-related variables emerged as the most influential predictors. The identification of patient-, surgeon- and transplant-centre-related predictors may inform future research into preoperative risk stratification and the development of clinically useful prediction tools.
