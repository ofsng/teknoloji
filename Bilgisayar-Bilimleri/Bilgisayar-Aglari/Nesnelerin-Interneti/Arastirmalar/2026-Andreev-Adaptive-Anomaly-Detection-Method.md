# Adaptive Anomaly Detection Method for Distributed Internet of Things Systems under Non-Stationary Traffic Conditions

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> This paper addresses the problem of adaptive cyber-threat detection in distributed Internet of Things systems operating under non-stationary network traffic and limited computational resources of edge nodes. The aim of the study is to improve anomaly and attack detection recall while reducing false positive probability in streaming data processing. The research task is to develop a streaming detection method based on a normal behavior profile formed from windowed statistical features, with confidence-based adaptation of profile parameters and the decision threshold. An adaptive statistical anomaly detection method is proposed that combines Z-score anomaly assessment, exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) adaptation of the normal profile, and dynamic threshold adjustment using a quantile of anomaly scores obtained from reliable normal windows. The confidence-based update mechanism restricts profile adaptation to windows classified as normal with sufficient confidence, thereby reducing the risk of incorporating anomalous states into the normal profile. Results. Experimental evaluation on an open Internet of Things network traffic dataset demonstrated an F1-score exceeding 0.98, perfect attack recall, and an average processing time below 0.25 ms per data window. The study also examined the influence of adaptation parameters, including the EWMA coefficient and quantile level, on normal-profile stability, detection sensitivity, and false-positive probability. The results indicate that confidence-based profile updating allows slow changes in background traffic to be tracked while limiting the incorporation of short-term anomalous states into the normal profile. The practical significance of the proposed method lies in its applicability to real-time cyber-threat monitoring and intrusion detection in distributed Internet of Things infrastructures, including edge nodes and gateways with limited computational resources, without the need for specialized hardware acceleration.
