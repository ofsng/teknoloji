# A Zero-Trust Architecture for Multinational Military Logistics Data Exchange: A Breach-Propagation Model of Coalition Cyber Resilience

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Multinational military operations depend on the exchange of logistics data across coalition partners whose information systems differ in assurance and whose mutual trust is necessarily incomplete. The research problem is that the prevailing perimeter model grants broad implicit trust once a partner is authenticated: a stolen credential, a compromised partner system or an insider can then move laterally across the shared logistics fabric towards the consolidated logistics picture. Policy asserts that a zero-trust architecture reduces this risk, but the size of the reduction has never been quantified for coalition logistics. The aim of the article is to measure the reduction in breach impact that zero-trust delivers in a coalition logistics data-exchange network, and how it depends on attacker capability. The method is a breach-propagation Monte-Carlo simulation of a six-nation network in which an adversary compromises one partner node and spreads; perimeter trust, microsegmentation and zero-trust are compared across a swept attacker-capability parameter. Zero-trust contains the breach: where the perimeter blast radius rises from 55% to 97% of nodes as the attacker improves, zero-trust holds it between 7% and 16%; the probability of reaching the critical consolidated store falls from up to 94% to below 6%; and the breach stays within roughly the originating nation instead of three to six of them. The contribution to logistics is quantified evidence that zero-trust turns a coalition-wide breach into a contained local incident, protecting the continuity of the shared logistics picture on which multinational sustainment depends, and the advantage grows as the adversary improves.
