# Green chemistry and biodegradable polymers: an environmental perspective

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> The rapid rise in plastic production and consumption has emerged as one of the most pressing environmental challenges of the twenty-first century. Unlike natural materials, conventional plastics are highly resistant to degradation and can persist in the environment for centuries, gradually breaking down into microplastics that contaminate oceans, agricultural soils, and even enter the human food chain. In response to this growing environmental threat, green chemistry has become a crucial strategy for rethinking material development, particularly within polymer science. Rooted in twelve guiding principles, green chemistry aims to design safer and more sustainable chemical products and processes, minimizing or eliminating hazardous substances throughout the entire lifecycle of materials. Its integration into polymer chemistry has notably transformed approaches to material design, synthesis, and disposal. This article provides a comprehensive review of biodegradable polymers through the lens of green chemistry, exploring their key classes, synthesis methods, physicochemical properties, and degradation pathways. Additionally, it discusses their current and potential applications across various industries, while addressing the scientific, economic, and regulatory challenges that must be overcome to fully realize their environmental and technological potential.
