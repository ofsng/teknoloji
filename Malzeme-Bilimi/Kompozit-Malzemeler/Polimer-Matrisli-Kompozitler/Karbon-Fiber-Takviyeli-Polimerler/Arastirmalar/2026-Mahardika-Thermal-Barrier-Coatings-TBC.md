# Thermal Barrier Coatings (TBC) For Carbon Fiber-Reinforced Polymer (CFRP) Composites: A Review

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Carbon fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP) composites are widely used in high-performance lightweight structures, yet their thermoset matrices begin to decompose at about 300°C, which limits their use in high-temperature environments. This article presents a comparative narrative review of two studies on thermal barrier coatings (TBCs) for CFRP developed by the same research group. The first study used a compression-molded Al2O3 plate mechanically bonded to CFRP through vacuum-assisted resin transfer molding (VARTM), whereas the second used a double-layered yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) TBC with graded porosity produced by flame spraying with PEEK particles as a pore former. Fabrication parameters, microstructure, thermal conductivity, thermal shock response, and residual flexural performance were extracted from both studies and compared. In the first study, a 1.45 mm TBC reduced the back-surface temperature of CFRP to 188–228°C under 500–700°C flames while retaining 90%, 62%, and 50% of the failure load, whereas uncoated CFRP lost 93–95% at 600–700°C. In the second study, a TBC about 0.66 mm thick with a thermal conductivity of about 0.3 W/m·K held the back surface at 230°C for 10 minutes under a 500°C flame, retaining 87% of flexural strength and 75% of modulus, while uncoated CFRP ignited. Both approaches are effective but rely on different protection mechanisms. Non-uniform test protocols, the absence of areal mass data, and the lack of long-term cyclic testing are the main limitations to be addressed in future work
