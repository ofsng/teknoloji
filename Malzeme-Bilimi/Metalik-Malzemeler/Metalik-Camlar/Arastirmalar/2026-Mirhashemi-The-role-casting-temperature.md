# The role of casting temperature on porosity formation and microstructure of suction-cast Zr-based bulk metallic glass

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> To produce high-quality bulk metallic glasses (BMGs), it is vital to use clean casting methods. However, quality can improve at the expense of properties. To overcome this trade-off dilemma, it is crucial to simultaneously investigate how casting parameters affect the quality and microstructure of BMGs, a topic that has rarely been addressed. Accordingly, X-ray radiography, field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis, and calorimetric characterization are used to investigate the impact of casting temperature on the porosity formation and microstructure of a Zr-based BMG. Three casting temperatures are selected by varying the melting currents corresponding to 280, 350, and 440 A. Increasing the casting temperature facilitates the formation of gas porosity. However, no micro-porosity is observed in areas that were free of porosity in the radiographic images of all as-cast BMGs, supporting a lack of significant gas in a vacuum atmosphere or coalescence of micro-porosities. As the casting temperature decreases, the enthalpy of relaxation of BMGs increases, indicating an increase in structural heterogeneity and a decrease in short-range order atomic clusters. This rejuvenation also manifests itself in the shift of the diffuse peak to a lower angle in XRD patterns, implying that the lower casting temperature is responsible for the higher cooling rate. Furthermore, a comparison is made between the current results and the inconsistent findings from recent studies. The origin of such a discrepancy, which can be due to casting method, casting equipment, and chemical composition of glassy alloy, is discussed.
