# Optical Scale-Up Networks for AI Clusters: A Review of Co-Packaged Optics and Emerging Light Sources

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> The rapid expansion of large-scale artificial intelligence (AI) training clusters has imposed unprecedented demands on intra-cluster interconnects. While scale-across and scale-out networks rely on optical transmission, the scale-up domain—governing chip-to-chip and accelerator-to-accelerator communication within a single rack or tightly coupled rack group—remains predominantly copper-based and is approaching fundamental physical limits. This review establishes a taxonomy of the three interconnect domains and identifies three physical bottlenecks—copper attenuation at 224 Gbps serializer/deserializer (SerDes) rates, the bandwidth-growth disparity between switch application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) capacity and the per-lane rate of pluggable transceivers, and front-panel density saturation—that together mandate an optical scale-up transition. We then survey the intermediate architectures—linear-drive pluggable optics (LPO), eXtra-dense pluggable optics (XPO) and near-packaged optics (NPO)—that extend separable optical modules into the 1.6 T and 3.2 T generations, before analyzing co-packaged optics (CPO) as the long-term solution and its stringent requirements on the optical source. Four candidate light-source families—continuous-wave distributed feedback (CW-DFB) lasers, quantum-dot (QD) comb lasers, vertical-cavity surface-emitting laser (VCSEL) arrays, and micro light-emitting diode (MicroLED) arrays—are benchmarked across wavelength scalability, thermal stability, relative intensity noise, power efficiency, and manufacturing yield. No single technology dominates all dimensions; the optimal choice is governed by transmission distance, thermal co-location constraints, and wavelength-multiplexing requirements.
