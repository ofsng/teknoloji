# Mapping the High-Field Response of Superconducting Nanowire Single-Photon Detectors

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) are increasingly being integrated into cryogenic quantum-sensing platforms that operate in large magnetic fields. Here, the high-field limits of these measurements were probed by characterizing the dark count response of a NbTiN SNSPD at up to 14~T using in situ control of the detector orientation and benchmarking that performance against normal detector operation in smaller magnetic fields. The detector performance is strongly anisotropic: perpendicular magnetic fields rapidly reduce the usable bias-current range, whereas near-parallel alignment enables operation at substantially larger total fields, with a finite operating margin retained to 12~T and measurable dark-count response at the 14~T field limit. The angular dependence is governed predominantly by the perpendicular component of the magnetic field, consistent with vortex-mediated suppression of the superconducting current scale. The same field-dependent current scale also determines the electrical pulse amplitude, linking high-field detector operation directly to readout performance. These results establish a practical route for deploying SNSPDs in multi-tesla quantum-sensing and high-field cryogenic experiments.
