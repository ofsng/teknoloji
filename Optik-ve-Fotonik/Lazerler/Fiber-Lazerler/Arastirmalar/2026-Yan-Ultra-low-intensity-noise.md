# Ultra-low-intensity noise broadband femtosecond pulse generation in an Er-doped all-fiber laser

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Broadband, low-noise mode-locked femtosecond fiber lasers are key enablers for advanced applications such as ultrafast imaging and high-precision photonic systems, where their spectral width and noise performance directly determine key metrics such as resolution and sampling accuracy. Although mode-locked fiber lasers operating in the near-zero dispersion regime can generate requisite broadband pulses with short pulse widths, their frequent reliance on free-space optics and susceptibility to heavy pump technical noise at low Fourier frequency render the realization of low-noise broadband pulses a significant challenge. We experimentally demonstrate a broadband, low-noise mode-locked Er-doped all-fiber laser using the nonlinear polarization rotation (NPR) scheme. Through effective intracavity dispersion and nonlinearity management, our all-fiber resonator directly produces pulses with a 3 dB bandwidth of 100.1 nm and a dechirped duration of 72 fs. Crucially, through polarization induced pump stabilization, we obtain an exceptionally flat and ultra-low relative intensity noise (RIN) profile below ∼−143 dBc/Hz across 100 Hz to 1 MHz. This excellent stability is corroborated by a low timing jitter of 16 fs integrated over the same Fourier frequency range. Our findings establish a new benchmark for robust, high-performance all-fiber ultrafast laser sources, paving the way for practical high-precision applications.
