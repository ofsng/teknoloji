# High-Temperature Water-to-Water Heat Pumps for the Retrofit of a Residential Complex

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Retrofitting existing residential complexes with heat pumps is limited by high-temperature distribution systems, investment cost and heat-source availability. This study proposes an integrated thermo-economic and environmental sizing of a hybrid aquifer-source water-to-water heat pump system for space heating and domestic hot water (DHW) preparation in a residential complex situated in Cluj-Napoca, Romania. The existing natural gas boilers are retained for peak loads. Heat pumps for heating (with R290) and for DHW preparation (with R290 or with R744), sized on hourly demand from typical climatic data and compressor-based COP correlations, can supply 87% of the annual heat demand. The existing gas boilers need to operate only at ambient temperatures below −4 °C. The annual COP for heating is 3.6 and for DHW preparation is 3.0 (for R290) or 4.4 (for R744). The aquifer must supply up to about 28 L/s, which must be confirmed by a hydrogeological investigation. CO2 emissions can decrease by 53.2% (for R290) and 57.5% (for R744). With the current high price of electricity and low price of natural gas in Romania, the investment is not feasible without financial support, but the situation is different in other countries. Economic viability is governed mainly by the electricity-to-gas price ratio.
