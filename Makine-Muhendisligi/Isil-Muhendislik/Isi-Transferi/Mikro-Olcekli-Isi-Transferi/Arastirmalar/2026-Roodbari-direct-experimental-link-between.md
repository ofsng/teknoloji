# A direct experimental link between three-phase contact line behavior and microscale heat transfer mechanisms beneath individual bubbles

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Pool boiling is one of the most important phase-change processes on the planet, a fact that has motivated its experimental, theoretical, and computational study for decades. Understanding the underlying physical processes at the microscopic level beneath individual bubbles is necessary to enable intelligent surface design and macroscopic performance optimization for a given application and working fluid. However, such experimental studies are challenging and thus far the causal links between the three-phase contact line (CL) and the thermal processes they influence have been largely inferred from thermal data rather than a direct, independent sensing of both the phase behavior and surface temperature underneath the bubble. This work combines a capacitance-based phase interface sensing scheme with microscale thermal sensors to unlock the mechanistic interdependence of dynamic phase interface behavior and heat transfer mechanisms beneath an individual nucleating bubble. Based on the experimental results, the thermal data clearly captures key events such as rewetting and bubble growth, each of which are able to be directly correlated to the movement of the three-phase CL as tracked independently by the capacitance sensors. Experimental results also show that the motion of the receding CL due to rewetting was more than three times faster than the advancing CL throughout bubble growth. Understanding the heat transfer phenomena associated with bubble nucleation facilitated by this research would have a widespread impact, including but not limited to supercritical boilers, high-power electronics components, ultra-high efficiency data centers, and economical, more reliable heat exchangers.
