# Wireless Passive LC Pressure Sensor for Implantable Hemodynamic Pressure Monitoring with Long Detection Distance

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Pulmonary artery pressure (PAP) monitoring is important for early detection of heart failure progression, but right heart catheterization is an invasive procedure and thus carries risks of bleeding, infection, vascular injury, and cardiac complications. Conversely, noninvasive methods provide indirect assessments and require sophisticated equipment. This paper presents the design and fabrication of a wireless passive LC pressure sensor for implantable pulmonary artery pressure (PAP) monitoring. The sensor comprises a pressure-sensitive capacitor manufactured using standard MEMS processes and anodic bonding technology, as well as a double-layer spiral inductor on a printed circuit board (PCB). The use of electrostatic shielding electrodes avoids the pull-in effect during anodic bonding, achieving a 2 µm spacing between the capacitor plates to enhance pressure sensitivity. In wireless testing, the LC pressure sensor exhibited a sensitivity of -8.7 kHz/mmHg over the 0–100 mmHg range, with a resolution approaching 1 mmHg. Furthermore, a readout method based on an 8-shaped receive coil and a circular transmit coil enables a readout distance of approximately 9 cm and enables real-time pressure monitoring with the LC pressure sensor placed in water through 5 cm-thick pork. This novel LC pressure sensor offers considerable potential for real-time PAP monitoring, thus contributing to the early-stage prediction of heart failure progression in patients.
