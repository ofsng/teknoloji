# Thin Water Layer Induced Signal Degradation in Autonomous FMCW Radar Systems

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Severe winter conditions may cause significant attenuation of Frequency-Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) automotive radar signals. The original contribution of this work is to analyze the attenuation caused by thin layers of pure and saline water, as well as the depth effect of these layers on automotive radar signal, through modelling, simulation, and measurements. In this context, we study electromagnetic wave propagation through an automotive Teflon radar radome in the 72–82 GHz band, focusing on a 77 GHz Long-Range Radar (LRR) used for adaptive cruise control. The total attenuation is evaluated by accounting for free space path loss, atmospheric attenuation, radome attenuation, rain-induced attenuation, and additional losses from water accumulation on the radome. The study employs a Double-Debye Dielectric Model (D3M) for water permittivity, combined with a multilayer Propagation Matrix Approach (PMA), and validates the results through numerical simulations (Ansys HFSS) and experimental measurements. The results show a signal degradation of 51.67 dB (two ways) over 0.25 km due solely to the presence of a thin water layer (0.80 mm thickness) on the radar radome, which explains why the radar is stopped.
