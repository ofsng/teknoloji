# Prediction of Oscillations in Nonlinear Circuits with Real Memristors via the Harmonic Balance Approach

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Emerging devices such as memristors are expected to play an ever increasing role in future electronics and for the implementation of brain-like neuromorphic computers. In this context, the study of memristor circuits implementing spike generators, oscillators or chaotic sources of signals is of particular importance. While the dynamic analysis of circuits with the ideal memristor model proposed by Chua has reached a fairly mature state, that of circuits with real memristors is in its infancy and no sufficiently systematic method is available. One difficulty is the complexity of real memristor models. The main goal of this paper is to develop an approximate analytic method to study the dynamics of nonlinear circuits with real memristors which is based on simplifying the memristor model. The method is applicable to a large class of real memristors with bipolar (digital) characteristics. To this end, the popular and widely used (V)TEAM Memristor Model (TMM) of real bipolar memristors is considered. Such memristors are subject to abrupt switches between two conductance values at certain threshold voltages. A technique is introduced to approximate via a simple hysteron the actual hysteresis loop displayed by the conductance of a TMM. This leads to the introduction of a new Hysteretic Memristor Model (HMM) which is much more simple from a mathematical viewpoint than TMM. One key advantage is that the dynamics of HMM circuits can be analyzed via well-established techniques as the Harmonic-Balance (HB) approach. Such technique would be difficult to use with TMM circuits due to the complexity of the equations describing their dynamic behaviors. The dynamics of some prototypical HMM circuits are analyzed by using the HB approach to predict the existence of cycles and oscillations. Then, it is verified through simulations that their dynamic behaviors closely approximate those of the corresponding TMM circuits.
