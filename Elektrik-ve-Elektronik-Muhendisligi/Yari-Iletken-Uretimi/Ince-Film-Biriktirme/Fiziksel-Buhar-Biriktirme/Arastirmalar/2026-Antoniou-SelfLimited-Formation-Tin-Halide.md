# Self‐Limited Formation of 2D Tin Halide Perovskite Layers by Sequential Physical Vapor Deposition

## 1. Araştırmanın özeti

> Two‐dimensional (2D) halide perovskites are emerging as materials to address the stability limitations of their three‐dimensional counterparts. Here, we demonstrate the room‐temperature formation of the 2D perovskite PEA 2 SnI 4 using sequential physical vapor deposition (PVD). films are first deposited and then exposed to phenylethylammonium iodide (PEAI), leading to a self‐limiting conversion of the near‐surface region into PEA 2 SnI 4. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) reveals significant recrystallization. A high degree of crystallization is corroborated via AFM where single molecular layer resolution is achieved. Photoluminescence (PL) measurements show strong excitonic emission at 1.97 eV and approximate PL quantum yield values of 0.3%. Surface‐sensitive X‐ray photoelectron spectroscopy measurements confirm the correct stoichiometry and the absence of an oxidation state, whereas bulk sensitive measurements show incomplete conversion, leaving a layer below the PEA 2 SnI 4. The PEA 2 SnI 4 thickness was limited to approximately 30 nm, which is attributed to the large organic cation that cannot penetrate the layer effectively. Longer PEAI exposures enhance optical quality but eventually lead to excess molecules on the surface. The PEA 2 SnI 4 capping layer reduces the decomposition rate of in containing atmospheres, underscoring its potential as a stabilizing treatment for Sn‐based perovskites.
